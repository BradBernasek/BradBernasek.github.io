Privacy Policy

The Body Guard app · Effective November 2, 2026

The Body Guard helps you take skin photos on a schedule and compare them over time. Those photos are about as private as it gets, so the app is built to keep them on your iPhone. This policy says exactly what the app stores, what (little) ever leaves your phone, and how to delete it all.

The short version

  • Your photos, labels, flags, notes and history stay on your iPhone. The developer never receives them.
  • The app has no server, no ads, no tracking, no analytics and no third-party code that sends data anywhere. Nothing is sold or shared for marketing.
  • The only thing that reaches the developer is a message you choose to send with Contact Us.
  • The app has no iCloud storage of its own. Your iPhone backup and iCloud Photos are Apple services that you control (see “Backups you control”).
  • You can delete everything from inside the app with Delete account.

Who is responsible

The Body Guard is made by Brad Bernasek (“the developer”, “we”). Questions about this policy go through Contact Us in the app or the support page.

What stays on your iPhone

WhatWhere it lives
Skin and full-body scan photosSaved to your Photos library as Hidden photos, so they sit behind Face ID in Photos (Albums › Hidden). The app also keeps its own copy inside the app so it can compare one photo with the next; iOS locks those files whenever the phone is locked.
Labels, flags, notes and historyKept inside the app on your iPhone and locked by iOS whenever the phone is locked. The app also keeps one extra file with your labels, flags and history, encrypted with your password and your Recovery Key. Without one of those, nobody can read that file, the developer included.
Your accountLocal to your iPhone. There is no server account. Face ID is handled by Apple; the app never sees your face data.
Scan schedule and remindersOn your iPhone. Reminders are local notifications, and the place you pin for full-body scan reminders stays on your iPhone.

Because these stay on the device, Apple’s definition of “collected” data does not apply to them, and none of it is sent to the developer.

Backups you control

iPhone backup. A normal iPhone backup (iCloud Backup or your computer) includes the app’s data: its photo copies, notes, scan history and the encrypted labels file. That backup is Apple’s service under your Apple Account settings; the app does not send it anywhere itself, and you can switch the app out of your iCloud Backup in your iPhone’s iCloud settings.

iCloud Photos. If you have iCloud Photos turned on, Apple syncs your Photos library, including Hidden photos, under your Apple Account settings. That is Apple’s service, set by you. The app itself does not upload anything to iCloud.

Backup folder (optional). In Settings (Privacy & Security › Backup) you can have the app copy a backup to a folder you choose (on your iPhone, a Mac or a cloud drive). Everything in it is encrypted with your password before it leaves the app. If the folder is on a cloud drive, that provider holds the encrypted files under its own policy.

Save a copy. You can export the encrypted labels file to a place you pick (Files, a Mac, a drive) and later use Restore from a copy. Where that copy goes is your choice; the app never sends it anywhere by itself.

What leaves your phone

Contact Us (only when you send it)

If you send a message with Contact Us, it includes your name, your account email address, any contact info you add and your message. If you choose Report a bug, it also includes the app version, your iPhone model and a short log of recent app activity. The log is cleaned before it is sent so it never names a body area, a photo or a file, and a message never includes your photos.

The message travels through a small Google Apps Script relay and arrives in the developer’s email. Google handles it only to deliver and store that email. It is used only to answer you and fix problems, and it is kept only as long as needed to help you. Ask through Contact Us and it will be deleted.

Apple services

Some features ask Apple for information. Those requests go to Apple, not to the developer, and Apple handles them under Apple’s own privacy policy.

  • Find care sends your current location to Apple Maps to list nearby doctors.
  • The widget’s UV reading sends your location, rounded to about 1 km, to Apple Weather.
  • The town on a My Moment asks Apple to turn your current location into a town name. Only the town name is saved, never the coordinates.
  • Doctor-arrival reminders ask Apple to turn the appointment address you typed into a point on the map.

Health Database Updates (only if you turn it on)

This is off unless you allow it. When it is on, about once a month and only when you are online, the app downloads one signed file of health reference values (such as UV levels and BMI ranges) from bradbernasek.com. Like any web request, this shows your device’s IP address to the website’s host. Nothing else is sent: no account, no photos, no results and no identifiers. The app uses the file only if its digital signature checks out. Turn it off any time in Settings › Health Database Updates; while it is off, the app makes no such requests.

Permissions and why

PermissionUsed for
CameraTaking your scan, spot and My Moment photos.
Photos (add only)Saving scan photos to your Photos library as Hidden photos. The app cannot see or browse the rest of your library.
Face IDUnlocking the app.
LocationOnly for the features you turn on: full-body scan reminders only when you are at the one place you pinned (for example, home) and inside the time window you set; a reminder when you arrive at your doctor’s office; the town name on a My Moment; Find care; and the widget’s UV reading. The developer never receives your location.
Motion & FitnessThe driving check in My Moments: the phone is asked whether you were in a moving vehicle in the last few minutes, so a My Moment never asks for a photo while you drive. The answer is used once on your iPhone and is not stored or sent.
NotificationsYour scan and check reminders.
CalendarAdding your skin appointments to your calendar and showing your schedule when you pick a time.
HealthReading your weight, height, BMI, activity rings and steps. Read-only: the app never writes to Health.
Microphone & Speech RecognitionTurning a Quick Note you dictate into text on your iPhone.

Every permission is optional. You can turn any of them off at any time in iPhone Settings › The Body Guard; the feature that needs it simply stops working.

What we never do

  • No advertising, tracking or analytics, and no data brokers.
  • No selling or renting your data, ever.
  • No sending your photos or health details to AI services or anyone else.

If the developer ever uses a service provider to handle your data, that provider must protect it at least as well as this policy does and may use it only for the job it was given.

Deleting your data

Delete account and all data in the app (Settings › Account) erases your account, photos, scans, notes, appointments and settings on this iPhone, including the encrypted labels file and the keys; the backup the app keeps on this iPhone; and your backup in the folder you chose, if you set one up.

The app can only add to your Photos library, not remove from it, so delete the scan photos yourself in the Photos app (Albums › Hidden). Copies you made with Save a copy are yours to delete wherever you saved them. Deleting the app also removes its on-device data, but older iPhone backups may keep the app’s data until those backups are replaced.

To have a Contact Us message deleted, or to ask what the developer holds about you (normally just that message), use Contact Us. You can withdraw any consent at any time by turning off the permission in iPhone Settings or deleting your account.

Age

The Body Guard is for adults 18 and over. It is not meant for children, and the developer does not knowingly receive any child’s information.

Not a medical device. The Body Guard does not diagnose anything. It helps you keep track of your skin. Always check with a doctor or dermatologist about any change, and before making any medical decision. In an emergency, call 911.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date. A change that affects what leaves your phone will also be shown in the app before it applies.
